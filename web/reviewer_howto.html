Each clinical association below was extracted from a published paper by an AI curation pipeline and linked to a specific microbial taxon. For each card, we would be grateful if you could assess two things: (1) the curator's evidence-level grade — feel free to accept it or choose a different one; and (2) whether the AI-written statement faithfully reflects what the paper actually says (Accurate / Overstated / Understated / Unsure).
Reviewer guidelines

Evidence level

How strongly the cited paper(s) support the claim — judged by study type, scale, and clinical relevance.

  • E3 — Strong: clinical practice guidelines, meta-analyses, systematic reviews, or multiple independent human cohorts within a single paper.
  • E2 — Moderate: a single human cohort, a single randomised controlled trial, a case-control study, or a cross-sectional human study.
  • E1 — Limited / preliminary: animal models, in vitro studies, case reports, or mechanistic work without human-cohort confirmation.
  • Undetermined: you cannot tell from the paper which level applies, or the cited evidence is too ambiguous to grade.

Quality of the curated statement

How faithfully the AI-written statement reflects what the paper actually says.

  • Accurate: the statement matches the paper's claims and scope. Nothing added, nothing weakened.
  • Overstated: the statement claims more than the paper supports — wider population, stronger causality, more certainty, or broader scope.
  • Understated: the statement claims less than the paper supports — leaves out a stronger finding, narrows a population, or softens a clearly established result.
  • Unsure: you cannot confidently judge. Please feel free to choose this rather than guessing — it is a perfectly valid answer.
3Vote on each claim

For every claim card: pick one Evidence button (pre-set to the curator's choice — accept or change) and one Quality button. Add a comment if you want to flag anything. Votes auto-save on every click.
